# Art direction — the origin cast (sponsor, debt-holder, rival apprentice)

**Status:** Direction brief, approved. Not final art — specs the silhouette, palette and
guild-affiliation treatment for whoever paints these three. Written for Phase 2 of the
origin-story brief (Origin A with a touch of C: a parent's failed Circuit run left a real debt).

**Reads with:** `docs/ART_BIBLE_GUILD_COLOURS.md` (the three-colour-system rule this brief must
not violate), `docs/ART_BIBLE_LOWPOLY.md` (§2 — where these portraits sit in the style split),
`docs/WORLD_GUILDS.md` (the eight guilds this brief casts all three roles against), `art.gd`
(`TEAM_COLOURS` / `TEAM_BADGES`, reused here on a different carrier).

---

## 0. Framing decisions

**This is a new asset class.** No existing bible covers human characters — only creatures and
venues. `ART_BIBLE_LOWPOLY.md` §2 puts "stable / market / report portraits" in the **painterly
2D** bucket, seen at ~320px with full attention. Treat all three roles as portraits in that
register: painted, not modelled, same paint quality as the 12 creature portraits, not the
low-poly battlefield tier.

**Guild affiliation reuses `TEAM_COLOURS` / `TEAM_BADGES`, but the carrier changes.**
`ART_BIBLE_GUILD_COLOURS.md` reserves the *sash* for a competitor currently fielding a match —
explicitly removable, swapped per team, per match. None of these three are wearing a match-day
sash in the sense the creature roster is (two of the three aren't competing at all). Same eight
colours, same eight badges, a **different carrier per role** — pin, stamp, apron trim, or (in
one case below) a sash worn deliberately off-field. This keeps the three-system palette rule
(league material / team colour / status) intact without overloading "sash" to mean two
different things.

## 1. Sponsor/mentor — Saddlers' Guild

**Guild pick and why:** `WORLD_GUILDS.md` names the Saddlers as the guild that "actively
recruits first-time Tamers... the guild most newcomers affiliate with first," youngest guild,
sport-native, closest to a modern sports-apparel outfit. The natural sponsor for a player
starting from a failed-run debt — practical, invested in the player succeeding, not
aristocratic patronage.

**Silhouette and archetype:** a retired or semi-retired competitor, now training. Reads as a
craftsman-coach, not a wizard-mentor — an old boxing trainer or a farrier who's seen thirty
seasons, not a robed sage. Weathered, practical build, no ceremonial posture. Working hands are
the tell — calloused, visibly used for the trade (they made sashes and wraps for years before
this was gear anyone rode into the ring).

**Distinguishing features from the creature roster's visual language:** human anatomy and
proportion separate the register on their own, but the specific thing to protect is **material
honesty** — the "athlete dressed for the ring, not for war" doctrine applies here in reverse:
this character is dressed for the *workshop*, not the ring. A satchel of dye pots, spare thread,
a measuring tape at the hip — visibly worn tools of the Saddlers' trade, not decoration.

**Guild affiliation carrier:** tan-leather apron with a stitched Saddlers ✚ badge at the chest,
or a leather cuff/wrist-guard in the same tone — a *worker's* mark, not a competitor's sash.
This is the rule that separates "person who trains" from "creature who competes" at a glance
for every future NPC of this type.

**Tone note specific to this character:** warm, not grand. A mentor with limited means (the
player's own debt implies a modest stable, not a wealthy patron) — avoid any cue that reads as
prosperous benefactor (fine cloth, jewellery, an ornate workshop). Worn but well-kept is the
target register.

## 2. Debt-holder — Assayers' Guild

**Guild pick and why:** the brief calls for "a guild ledger... a face, not an abstraction," and
the Assayers' Guild literally keeps the ledgers — `WORLD_GUILDS.md`: "grading everyone else's
goods; keeps the ledgers," "precise, self-important, the oldest and most respected guild." This
does the sympathetic-neutral work for free: the Assayers have no stake in the player's failure
or success, only in the record being accurate. A fundamentally different energy from a loan
shark.

**This is the design trap named in the brief — the direction has to work against every reflex a
"debt collector" character invites:**

| avoid (villain-coded) | use instead (guild-professional-coded) |
|---|---|
| dark palette, shadow, cloak | chalk white / pale, well-lit — the Assayers' own colour is nearly the brightest livery in the set |
| looming posture, staring down | level eye contact, seated at a desk or standing beside the player, not over them |
| sneer, sharp/angular face | neutral-to-kind expression; the tension lives in the PAPERWORK, not the face |
| a weapon or threatening prop | a ledger, a stamp, a scale — tools of *measurement*, not menace |
| ornate/wealthy dress (villain-as-aristocrat) | plain, precise guild dress — a uniform of exactness, not luxury |
| isolated/dark setting | a bright, orderly office — shelves of ledgers, good light, almost boring |

**Silhouette and archetype:** a civil clerk or exacting quartermaster, not a moneylender. Neat,
symmetrical dress, nothing flamboyant. The visual argument for sympathetic-or-neutral is that
this person's whole worldview is fairness-by-the-book — inflexible because the ledger is
inflexible, not because they're cruel. Reads as *tired precision*, not menace: someone who has
stamped a thousand debts and finds no satisfaction in any of them, including this one.

**Distinguishing features from the creature roster:** no gear-grammar overlap needed for a
human character, but the positive version of the rule: this character should look like they
belong in the same building as the Assay Table's ledgers (§1, `WORLD_GUILDS.md`) —
administrative, not confrontational.

**Guild affiliation carrier:** a wax-stamp badge (the ✦ glyph) worn as a lapel pin or pressed
into a ledger's cover, chalk-white piping on an otherwise plain coat. The stamp itself is a
strong recurring prop — the physical object that makes a debt "real" in this world (per §4,
`WORLD_GUILDS.md`: standing is *recorded*, not assumed) — and can reappear as the visual beat
when the debt resolves.

## 3. Rival apprentice — Dyers' Guild (recommended)

Two credible picks from `WORLD_GUILDS.md`, both "showman" temperaments that contrast well
against a Saddlers-sponsored player:

- **Dyers' Guild** (plum, ★) — "theatrical, colourful... the guild that actually started the
  custom of wearing team colours." The showiest possible rival, with a built-in irony: the
  guild that invented the sash convention fields the flashiest apprentice.
- **Glaziers' Guild** (bottle green, ■) — "vain, image-conscious, obsessed with reading clean,
  genuinely good at putting on a show." Cooler, more polished-technical than theatrical.

**Recommendation: Dyers.** Plum reads more distinct against the Saddlers' tan-leather
(warm-neutral vs. cool-jewel) than green does, and "the guild that invented showing off your
colours" gives the rival a built-in reason to be visually louder than the player without
contradicting the sport's no-villain framing — a peer with a different guild's temperament,
papered the same season, not an enemy.

**Silhouette and archetype:** same apprentice tier as the player-Tamer — young, unproven,
working gear rather than a veteran's worn kit — but *performed*. Where the player's kit should
read modest and practical, the rival's kit is the same functional pieces (measuring tape,
satchel, wraps) styled with visible pride — cleaner cut, a flourish in how the sash is tied,
better-kept boots. The contrast is presentation, not power.

**Distinguishing features from the creature roster:** same human/creature separation as the
other two roles; the thing to nail here is that this is the one role who would plausibly wear a
sash-like garment even off the field (a Dyer's apprentice showing off colourfastness is core to
their guild identity) — the one human role where a competitor-style sash accent is correct
rather than a mismatch. Worth stating explicitly so it isn't miscategorised as "wrong carrier"
against the mentor/debt-holder pattern above.

**Guild affiliation carrier:** a plum sash or scarf, worn with visible intention (tied a
specific way, a small flourish) plus the ★ badge as a pin — the one case where the
competitor-grade carrier is the right call, specifically because Dyers' identity is built
around wearing colours loudly.

## 4. Tone palette — opening scene (Town, week 0, empty barn)

Anchor: `ART_BIBLE_LOWPOLY.md`'s standing lighting statement, which has survived every art
direction change so far — **"a craftsman's yard after the day's work, lit by one warm working
lamp."** Target for this scene specifically, not a generic town palette.

- **Light:** single warm key (lamp, not sun — end of day, indoors/half-indoors), cool
  blue-grey ambient fill from an open barn door or high window. The same warm-key/cool-bounce
  split already specified for the battlefield lamp system — reuse it rather than inventing a
  separate mood.
- **Saturation:** low. A stake-setting scene, not a spectacle — nothing in frame should be as
  saturated as a guild livery colour. Save the palette's punch for when guild colours are
  introduced in later scenes (mentor, rival) so this scene reads as the quiet "before."
- **Palette:** worn wood, straw, dust — warm neutrals and desaturated browns/greys. Empty
  stalls read as *space waiting to be filled*, not decayed or abandoned — modest, not grim.
  Avoid: cracked timber, cobwebs, broken fixtures (reads as poverty/decay, wrong register);
  also avoid anything triumphant (no dawn light, no hero backlight, no wide golden glow) — the
  debt is real but this is a beginning, not a low point being dramatized.
- **Composition note:** if the mentor appears in this scene, their tan-leather accent is the
  first spot of guild colour the player sees — let it be the single warmest, most saturated
  element in an otherwise muted frame, so it reads as "someone is here for you" without the
  scene needing dialogue to say so.
