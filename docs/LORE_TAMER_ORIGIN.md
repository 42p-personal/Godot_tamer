## Overview

The player's parent ran a stable — **Second Sons**, unaffiliated, never guild-chartered — and
took a loan from a rival outfit, **Hidebound** (Tanners-flavoured, per `WORLD_GUILDS.md` §6),
to fund a real shot at the mid-ladder. The partnership came up short; the loan went unpaid; the
debt was recorded, plainly and without malice, in the Assayers' ledger the way every guild
records every unmet obligation. **Apprentice papers are the fastest legitimate way to clear it
— not because the player must, but because they choose to finish something that was never
theirs to finish.** That distinction is load-bearing and should survive every beat below: no
beat should read as the player being forced, cursed, or fated. They keep climbing because they
decided to.

The debt is never dressed as a threat. It is paperwork, plainly stated, in a world where
nobody's standing is automatic (`WORLD_GUILDS.md` §4) — a batch isn't graded, a journeyman
isn't a master, and a debt isn't cleared, until the ledger says so.

## The cast

- **The player's parent** — never appears on-page in the present day; referenced only.
  Ran Second Sons, an unaffiliated stable, to a real but unremarkable failure — not
  disgrace, just short. What "short" means is a Bronze-league beat, not a Wood-league one;
  see below.
- **Hidebound** — the rival stable holding the debt. Tanners-adjacent in temperament
  (scrappy, proud, quick to feel looked down on — `WORLD_GUILDS.md` §2), not Tanners-
  chartered. Proprietor unnamed here; writer's call.
- **The Hidebound apprentice** — Hidebound's own newly papered Tamer, licensed the same
  season as the player. Recurring foil across all eleven beats — a mirror, not a monster.
  Name deferred to writer.
- **A ledger clerk at the Assayers' Guild** — the recurring voice of the debt itself:
  procedural, unbothered, never sinister. Appears wherever the ledger needs to be read
  aloud. Name deferred to writer.

## Wood — *Canon: Provisional*

The Saddlers' Guild takes the player on — apprentice papers, the guild that "actively
recruits newcomers" per established canon (`WORLD_GUILDS.md` §2, restating Established fact).
A Saddlers clerk mentions, almost in passing, that the family name already has a line in the
ledger. First sighting of Hidebound's colours across the yard: their own new apprentice,
papered the same week. Costs nothing but curiosity — the player isn't told the number yet.

## Copper — *Canon: Provisional*

First away match, first real purse. A clerk at the Assayers' table — the same one from Wood
or a colleague — reads the debt out plainly when the player asks: a fixed sum, Second Sons,
owed to Hidebound, no interest accruing, no deadline attached beyond "the ledger doesn't
forget." Not a threat — the guild world doesn't do threats, it does records. The player
now knows exactly what they're climbing toward.

## Tin — *Canon: Provisional*

First direct meeting with the Hidebound apprentice — a shared card, or a corridor
introduction before either team's match. Cordial, competitive, a little pointed: Hidebound's
apprentice knows the family name too. Establishes the rivalry as personal-competitive, never
personal-hostile — two apprentices measuring themselves against each other, which is exactly
what the Circuit is for.

## Bronze — *Canon: Provisional*

The player meets someone who remembers Second Sons — a groundskeeper, a fellow stablehand,
a retired trainer working the concessions. What "failed" actually meant gets said plainly for
the first time: the parent's partnership came up short at a real but unremarkable rung, not a
dramatic collapse. This is the emotional floor of the whole arc and it should stay small —
a Bronze-league conversation, not a revelation.

## Iron — *Canon: Provisional*

The ledger becomes a real cost, not just a number: a portion of the player's purses is now
visibly earmarked against the debt (mechanism TBD by systems-designer — this doc only
requires that it read as procedural, never punitive). First genuine friction: does the
player resent carrying a debt that isn't theirs, or has it become their own project by now?
Leave that read to the writer/player, not decided here.

## Silver — *Canon: Provisional*

A direct exchange with Hidebound's proprietor — not a confrontation, a negotiation. Hidebound
proposes settling the balance differently: a wagered match, stable against stable, at Gold.
This is the pivot from "debt as background fact" to "debt as a storyline with a shape" — and
it's Hidebound's move, not the player's, keeping the player's agency intact (they can accept
or keep paying it down the slow way; either should be legitimate).

## Gold — *Canon: Provisional*

The wagered match, if accepted at Silver: player's team against Hidebound's, purse (or a
chunk of the standing debt) on the result. Framed exactly as `ART_BIBLE_GUILD_COLOURS.md`
demands — a sport fixture, not a duel. Win or lose, this is the emotional high point of the
lower half of the ladder; the debt visibly moves for the first time.

## Platinum — *Canon: Provisional*

The ledger entry closes — stamped, dated, witnessed by an Assayer, the same plain procedural
act as every other piece of standing in this world (`WORLD_GUILDS.md` §4, restating
Established fact). Whether the Gold wager cleared it outright or merely closed the gap is a
narrative-director call; either way, Platinum is where "the parent's debt" stops being the
throughline and "the player's own record" starts being the one that matters.

## Masters — *Canon: Provisional, restates Established framing (§5)*

Masters is graduation, not a harder Platinum (`WORLD_GUILDS.md` §5, Established). The Assay
Table's recognition here doubles as closure on the family name: the ledger's old Second Sons
entry gets a formal annotation — resolved, not erased. The Hidebound apprentice, likely also
Masters-track by now, offers something closer to respect than rivalry. The story's register
shifts from "clearing a debt" to "being recognised."

## Tamer Elite — *Canon: Provisional, restates Established framing (§5)*

Per established canon, Tamer Elite is an invitation to judge, not just to compete
(`WORLD_GUILDS.md` §5). Thematically: the player is now the kind of Tamer the guild world
trusts to grade *other* people's work — the exact authority a debt-shadowed apprentice
started nowhere near having. The debt is fully past tense by now; this beat is about what the
player has become instead, deliberately not about the parent anymore.

## Tamers Apex — *Canon: Provisional, restates Established framing (§5)*

Per established canon, this is the guilds jointly declaring the partnership **the Standard**
(`WORLD_GUILDS.md` §5, Established — the game's actual ending per `CLAUDE.md`). The closing
beat: the family name that once sat as a short, unremarkable line in a ledger now sits at the
top of it — not because the player finished what their parent couldn't (that would smuggle
destiny back in), but because they chose, of their own will, to finish something regardless.
Keep this line sharp in whatever text the writer produces: earned, not inherited.
